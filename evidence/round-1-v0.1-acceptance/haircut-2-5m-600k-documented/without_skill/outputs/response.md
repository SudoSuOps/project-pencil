**The $600K is itself a ceiling, not a floor.** Before conceding any of it, I'd check:

- **Whose bids they are.** Both numbers come from the buyer's side. Get your own roofer and mechanical contractor to price the same scope.
- **Replacement versus repair.** If the roof has five years left, or the units can be repaired or replaced in phases, the buyer is asking you to pay for a new roof they'll enjoy for 20 years. A fair credit is the cost of the deferred portion, not full replacement.
- **Who pays under the leases.** If tenants are responsible for HVAC, or roof costs are recoverable through CAM, the buyer's real exposure is far smaller than $600K.
- **What was already known.** If roof and HVAC age were disclosed in the OM, or obvious on a tour, the buyer priced them at LOI and this is a second bite.

**Make them itemize the $1.9M.** There are only a few things it can be:

- **Other diligence findings** (environmental, structural, title, estoppels, rent roll discrepancies). These are legitimate if documented, so ask for the reports.
- **Capitalized NOI.** This is only valid if diligence turned up income or expense numbers that differ from what you represented.
- **Market, financing or appraisal problems.** Rates moved, the lender cut proceeds, or the appraisal came in short. That is a retrade, not a diligence issue, and should be negotiated as one.
- **Anchoring.** They ask for $2.5M hoping to settle at $1.2M.

**How I'd respond:**

1. Ask in writing for a line-item breakdown with backup for the full $2.5M, and say you'll respond only to documented items.
2. Counter on the roof and HVAC alone, at a number below $600K once your own bids and the lease review are in.
3. Offer a closing credit or a repair escrow rather than a price cut, or offer to do the work yourself before closing if your pricing is better.

How hard you can hold depends on things I don't have: whether the deposit has gone hard, when the diligence period expires, whether you have a backup buyer, and how badly you need this close. If the buyer can still walk free and you have no backup, the $1.9M is a negotiation about your alternatives rather than about the building.
