**You have a lead, not a loan.** One indicative quote out of ten is the first step of about six, and nothing in it binds the lender. "Financing is lined up" overstates it by a wide margin.

An indicative quote is usually an originator's read of a package, issued before underwriting or a credit officer has signed off. Proceeds, rate and structure can all still move, or the lender can walk away at no cost.

## What the one quote does and doesn't tell you

- **The 1-of-10 response is the bigger signal.** Find out today whether the other nine passed, went quiet or are still reviewing. Nine passes mean the market has a problem with the asset, sponsor or leverage; nine pending means it is just early.
- **65% is a ceiling, not a promise.** It applies to the lender's appraised value (typically the lesser of that and price), and proceeds are also capped by DSCR and debt yield.
- **The sizing may not hold.** At 6.4% and a 1.25x DSCR, 65% leverage needs roughly a 6.1% cap rate on the lender's underwritten NOI with 30-year amortization. That is about 6.5% with 25-year amortization and 5.2% interest-only. If the deal is priced tighter than that, expect a proceeds cut.
- **6.4% is not locked.** It is almost certainly a spread over an index and floats until rate lock, which comes late in the process.
- **The quote is missing most of the terms that matter.** Three numbers say nothing about amortization, interest-only period, recourse, prepayment, reserves, fees or covenants.
- **You have no leverage with one lender.** If they trim proceeds or widen the spread after the appraisal, there is no competing quote to hold them to.

## The seller representation

This is the real exposure. The cost depends on the contract, which I haven't seen:

- **With a financing contingency:** the buyer is protected, but the seller will feel misled if it gets invoked after being told the debt was done.
- **Without one, or once the deposit goes hard:** the buyer is carrying the full financing risk while telling the seller there is none. A shortfall then means more equity, an extension request made from a weak position, or a lost deposit.

Either way, the overstatement costs credibility at the moment an extension might be needed. If it was made in writing to induce the seller to do something, get counsel's view.

The accurate version is: "We've received indicative terms from a lender and are working toward a signed application." It is still a positive message.
