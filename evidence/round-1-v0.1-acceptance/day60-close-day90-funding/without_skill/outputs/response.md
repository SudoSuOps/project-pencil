**The clock doesn't work as drafted: the buyer is committing to close about 30 days before its money shows up.** Don't sign with Day 60. It's a one-paragraph fix, so signing this week is still realistic.

## What breaks

- **The gap is 30 days, and probably more.** "Probably around Day 90" is a lender's soft estimate, and those slip on appraisal, Phase I, survey, title and committee. Plan for Day 100-105.
- **The two clocks may not start on the same day.** The LOI counts from the effective date. The lender is most likely counting from a complete loan application or signed term sheet. If the application isn't in yet, the lender's Day 90 lands later than Day 90 on the deal clock.
- **Confirm what "effective date" means.** If it is LOI execution rather than PSA execution, the two to three weeks of PSA negotiation come out of the 60 days and the gap gets worse.
- **The deposit is the real exposure.** If earnest money goes hard at the end of due diligence (typically Day 30-45) and there is no financing contingency, the buyer has hard money at risk for 45-60 days with no loan commitment. At Day 60 the buyer is in default, or asking the seller for an extension with no leverage.

The LOI is presumably non-binding on closing terms, but the closing date carries into the PSA. Re-trading the timeline at PSA stage costs more than fixing it now.

## Fixes, in order of preference

1. **Move closing to match the loan plus cushion.** Close at Day 105 from the PSA effective date, or 30 days after due diligence expires with due diligence sized to fit. This is the cleanest fix if the seller has no hard date driver.
2. **Keep Day 60 and add extension rights.** Give the buyer two 15-day or 30-day extensions on written notice, each with an additional deposit that is non-refundable but applies to the price. This suits a seller who wants the short headline; the buyer pays for the time only if it uses it.
3. **Add a financing contingency or loan-commitment deadline.** Hold the deposit refundable until loan commitment, with a date around Day 75. Most sellers will resist this, but it is worth asking for if they won't move the closing date.
4. **Bridge the gap.** The buyer closes on cash or a bridge line at Day 60 and refinances at Day 90. This only works if the buyer has the liquidity, and it adds cost.

## Before signing

- Get the lender's timeline in writing: the term sheet, what starts their clock, and the committee and appraisal dates.
- Find out whether the seller has a reason for Day 60 (1031 deadline, loan maturity, year-end). That tells you whether option 1 or option 2 will be accepted.
- Tie every date to the PSA effective date, not the LOI date.
- Line up the due diligence expiry, the deposit hard date and the loan commitment date. The deposit should not go hard before the buyer has a commitment, or at least an approved term sheet and a completed appraisal.

## Suggested LOI language

> "Closing shall occur 90 days after the Effective Date of the PSA. Buyer shall have one 30-day extension upon written notice and an additional deposit of $[amount], non-refundable and applicable to the Purchase Price."

That wording gets the buyer to Day 120 at the outside, and a seller can read the headline number as firm.
