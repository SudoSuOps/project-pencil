**The clock doesn't work as drafted: fix the closing date before anyone signs.** Closing is Day 60 and the money is "probably" Day 90, so the buyer is short by 30 days on the lender's own estimate, with no cushion.

**Check the anchors first.** The two numbers may not be counting from the same day:

- **Day 60** runs from the "effective date". If that means the PSA effective date (the usual convention), and the PSA takes 2-3 weeks to paper, real closing is roughly Day 75-80 from today.
- **Day 90** runs from whatever the lender meant: application, signed term sheet, or today. If the application isn't in yet, Day 90 hasn't started.

If the lender's clock is already running and the 60 days run from PSA signing, the true gap may be 10-15 days. If both start at signing, it is the full 30 or more.

**Why it matters even if the LOI is non-binding.** The LOI closing date becomes the PSA closing date. If the PSA carries Day 60 and the deposit goes hard before funding, the buyer is in default at Day 60 and the deposit is at risk. The seller gets a failed closing or a forced extension negotiated under pressure.

**Ways to fix it, in the order I'd try them:**

1. **Move closing to match the loan.** Set it at Day 100-105 from the PSA effective date: lender's Day 90 plus 10-15 days of buffer, since "probably" from a lender usually slips.
2. **Keep Day 60 and add extension rights.** Give the buyer one or two 30-day extensions, each with an additional deposit that is non-refundable but applies to the price. The seller keeps a short headline date and gets paid for the delay.
3. **Tie closing to loan approval.** For example, closing is 15 days after the financing contingency is waived, with an outside date around Day 105-120. Sellers resist open-ended financing outs, so this needs the outside date.
4. **Bridge the gap.** The buyer closes on cash or a bridge loan at Day 60 and refinances at Day 90. This only works if the buyer really has the liquidity.

**Before signing this week:**

- Get the lender's timeline in writing, including what Day 90 counts from and what is still outstanding (appraisal, environmental, survey, committee date).
- Define "effective date" explicitly in the LOI.
- Line up the due diligence expiry and the date the deposit goes hard against the loan commitment date, not just against closing. The deposit should not go hard before the buyer has a commitment.

Signing this week is still realistic, because this is a one-line change to the closing term plus an extension clause. Signing as drafted is what I'd advise against.

Which side are you on? If you're the seller, I'd push option 2. If you're the buyer, option 1 or 3.
