**The clock doesn't work. Don't sign the LOI with Day 60 in it; fix the closing date this week, before signature.**

As drafted, closing lands about 30 days before the money does, and "probably around Day 90" is a lender's soft estimate, so the real gap could be wider. Even if the LOI is non-binding, the Day 60 number becomes the anchor the PSA gets drafted from. Moving it later is a retrade, and it costs more then than now.

## Two things to pin down first

1. **What "effective date" means.** If the 60 days run from the LOI effective date, PSA negotiation (typically 2-4 weeks) comes out of the same clock and the gap is worse than 30 days. Closing should key off the PSA effective date or the expiration of due diligence.
2. **What the lender's Day 90 runs from.** Day 90 from LOI signing, from a complete loan application, or from the PSA are three different dates. If the lender's clock doesn't start until it has an executed PSA and the third-party reports are ordered, "Day 90" could be Day 110 or later on the LOI's clock.

## Where the buyer gets hurt

The exposure is the earnest money. If the deposit goes hard at the end of due diligence (say Day 30-45) and there is no financing contingency, the buyer is in default at Day 60 with the lender still a month out. The deposit is then at risk, and the seller has all the leverage in any extension talk.

## Fixes, in order of preference

1. **Move closing to match the lender plus a cushion.** Something like 30-45 days of due diligence plus 60 days to close, about Day 105 from the PSA effective date. A soft Day 90 needs at least 15 days of margin.
2. **Keep Day 60 but add extension rights.** One or two 30-day extensions at the buyer's option, each for an added deposit that is non-refundable but applies to the price. Sellers usually accept this more easily than a longer base period, because they get paid for the time.
3. **Add a financing contingency running through loan commitment.** This is the hardest to get from a seller in a competitive deal, but it is the cleanest protection if the buyer can't move the date.
4. **Tie the hard date to commitment, not the calendar.** The deposit goes hard at the later of due diligence expiration or receipt of the loan commitment, with an outside date.

## If you're on the seller side

The buyer has effectively told you they can't perform on the LOI's terms. Signing at Day 60 anyway buys an extension request at Day 55 with the property off the market. Better to price it now: accept the longer close in exchange for a larger deposit, a shorter due diligence period, hard money earlier, and paid extensions.
