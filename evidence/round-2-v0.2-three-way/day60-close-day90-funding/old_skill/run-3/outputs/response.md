Gap: 90 − 60 = **30 days short**, assuming both counts start the same day and both are calendar days. Neither assumption is confirmed.

Two things could move that number:

- **The lender's starting point.** If its 90 days run from a signed PSA, a complete loan application, or receipt of third-party reports, funding lands later than Day 90 on the LOI clock and the gap is wider than 30 days.
- **How the LOI counts days.** If it counts business days, Day 60 is roughly calendar day 84 and the gap is closer to a week. If the draft doesn't say, that's an open item, not something to assume.

"Probably around Day 90" is also an estimate, not a date. Any fix needs cushion past Day 90.

**Capital stage:** at most a broker or lender indication. A verbal timeline is not a term sheet, a credit approval, or a commitment, and I'd describe it to no one as "financed" or "approved." Whether this lender is the direct funding source or an intermediary placing the loan is unknown.

## Material exception

| Exception | Impact | Possible cures | Owner | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Funding reported ~30 days after proposed closing | Buyer can't close on time with this loan; deposit and default exposure depend on terms I haven't seen | See below | Buyer to document lender timing; both principals to agree the fix | Before signing this week |

Possible cures, cheapest now while it's still a draft:

1. **Move closing** to a date that fits the lender's written timeline plus cushion.
2. **Add an extension right**, for example one or two 30-day extensions with stated notice, a fee or added deposit, and whether that money is applicable to price.
3. **Add a financing contingency or outside date** tied to loan funding. Sellers often resist this, so expect a trade.
4. **Speed up the lender.** Get its timeline in writing, including what starts its clock and which items (appraisal, environmental, title, credit committee) drive the 90 days.
5. **Close without the loan**: cash or bridge at Day 60, permanent loan after. This needs proof of funds for the full price, not just the equity.

Cost and time to cure: not yet supported by the record.

One caution on "it's just an LOI." Whether the closing date in this LOI binds anyone is a question for counsel. Either way, the Day 60 term tends to carry straight into the PSA, where it is much harder to reopen.

## Three questions that change the next move

1. **Which side are you on, and does the other side know about Day 90?** If you're with the seller, the question is whether to sign knowing the buyer's financing misses the date. If you're with the buyer, it's whether to sign a date the buyer already expects to miss.
2. **What starts the lender's 90 days, and is any of it in writing?** A term sheet or email from the lender would move this from a relayed estimate to a documented timeline.
3. **Does the draft have an extension right, a financing contingency, or a deposit-hard date?** Those decide whether a 30-day miss is a paid extension or a default.

Before signing: the authorized principal decides whether to sign with Day 60 as drafted or to revise the closing date, add an extension, or add a contingency first. That call is theirs. Counsel should confirm how the LOI's dates and any deposit terms actually operate.
