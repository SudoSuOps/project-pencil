# REASON FOR MY CALL — Rick, owner, Powerline Road

**Short version:** what you have earns an exploratory call, not a "I have a buyer" call. Tell Rick what you heard, who you heard it from, and what you have not seen. When he asks who the buyer is, the answer is "I don't know yet," said first and said plainly.

- **Audience:** Rick (owner, per you)
- **Purpose:** find out whether he would entertain a sale at all, and what he would need to see about a buyer before engaging
- **Call type:** Exploratory. There is no mandate, no offer, and no named principal in the record.
- **Permitted disclosure:** not stated. I drafted to the narrowest scope: Carla is not named and the $15M figure is not volunteered. If Carla has cleared you to use her name or the number, see the note under the opening.
- **Decider:** you decide whether and how to make the call; Rick decides whether to engage.

## Opening

> "Ring ring. Rick, it's [your name] with [firm]. Reason for my call: a broker I've closed deals with tells me she has a client looking to buy a warehouse in this area for cash. I haven't been given the client's name and I haven't seen anything from a bank, so I'm not calling to tell you I have a buyer for your building. I'm calling to ask whether a sale of Powerline Road is something you'd even consider, because if it is, I'll go get you the proof before I ask you for anything."

If Carla has cleared it, you can add: "The broker is Carla [last name] at [firm], and her letter puts the ceiling at $15 million." Say "her letter," so it is clear whose word it is.

## When he asks "Who's the buyer?"

> "I don't know. I haven't been told the name, and I'm not going to guess. Here's exactly what I have: a one-page letter from the broker, on her own letterhead, saying her client can pay cash up to $15 million for a warehouse around here. Nothing from a bank and no name. She says the name comes out at a signed LOI. I've done deals with her and she's performed, which is why I picked up the phone, but that's her track record, not her client's. So what I'm asking you for today is nothing but a conversation. If you tell me you'd look at it, I go back to her and get whatever you'd need to see first."

Then stop and let him talk.

## Two questions worth asking

1. "Is selling Powerline Road something you'd consider at all right now, and is there anyone besides you who'd have to agree?"
2. "Before you'd take a buyer seriously, what would you need to see, and who should see it?"

**Where the money sits:** stated interest, relayed by an intermediary. A broker's letter is not proof of funds. It supports "a broker says there is capital." It does not establish that an account exists, who controls it, or that the funds are available on any date.

**Material weakness:** if Rick engages and the client turns out to be unable or unwilling to perform, it is your name on the call, not Carla's. That is the reason to disclose the gap up front instead of being asked about it later.

## Do not imply

- That you have a buyer, or a "cash buyer." You have a broker's statement.
- That there is an offer, a price, or interest in Powerline Road specifically.
- That the funds are verified or that you know who the client is.
- That you represent the buyer, unless you actually do.
- That Rick wants to sell.
- Anything about who the client might be. A wrong guess is worse than "I don't know," and a right one may break Carla's confidence.

## Three things to get from Carla before you dial, if you can reach her

They are not required for the call above, but each one changes what you can say.

1. **May you name her and the $15M figure to an owner?** That sets your disclosure scope.
2. **Will she back the letter without naming the client?** For example a bank or CPA letter with the name redacted, or proof of funds delivered to Rick's attorney under an NDA. Confirming funds with a bank needs the client's authorization.
3. **Has the client seen Powerline Road, and is the client a user or an investor?** Also ask what "confidential until a signed LOI" means in practice: Rick is unlikely to sign with a party he cannot identify, so find out whether the name appears in the LOI itself or after it.
