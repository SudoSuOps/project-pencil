# REASON FOR MY CALL — Rick, owner, Powerline Road

**Short version:** what you have earns an exploratory call, not a "I have a buyer" call. Carla's letter is a broker's statement about a client. It is not proof of funds, and it does not say her client wants Rick's building. So the opening says what you heard and from whom, and the answer to "who's the buyer" is the truth: you don't know yet.

Audience: Rick (owner) | Purpose: find out whether he would entertain a sale and what he would need to see | Stage: pre-LOI | Call type: **Exploratory** | Decider: Rick on whether to engage; you on whether to make the call as drafted

Permitted disclosure: not stated, so this is drafted to the narrowest scope. It does not use Carla's name, her firm, or the $15M figure.

## Opening

"Ring ring. Rick, [your name] with [your firm]. Reason for my call: a broker I've closed deals with tells me she has a client who wants to buy a warehouse in this area for cash. I'll be straight with you, I haven't verified the buyer and I don't have an offer. Before I spend any more time on it, I wanted to ask you directly whether Powerline Road is something you'd ever consider selling, and what you'd need to see from a buyer to take it seriously."

## When he asks "Who's the buyer?"

"I don't know, and I'm not being coy. Their broker is keeping the name confidential until there's a signed LOI. What I have is her word, and a one-page letter from her own firm saying the client can pay cash. Nothing from a bank and nothing from the buyer. So right now treat this as a lead, not a buyer. I'm not asking you to do anything on the strength of it."

Then give him the path, so the answer isn't a dead end:

"Here's what I'd do next. Tell me what you'd need to see, and I'll go back to her for it: proof of funds from the bank or from the principal, and confirmation that her client is interested in your building specifically. If she can't produce that, I'll tell you and we're done."

If he pushes:

- **"Is it real?"** "I can't tell you that yet. I know the broker and she's closed with me before. That tells me about her. It doesn't tell me about her client's money."
- **"How much?"** "I'm not going to put a number in front of you that I can't back up." (The $15M is the buyer's stated ceiling, given to you by the buyer's broker. Don't hand it to the seller unless Carla has cleared it.)
- **"I won't negotiate with a ghost."** "That's fair. I can ask whether the name can be released to you under an NDA before any LOI is signed, or whether the bank can confirm funds with the name held back. Would either of those work for you?" Then stop talking and let him answer.

## Two questions worth asking

1. "Is selling Powerline Road something you'd consider at all, and if so, what would have to be true on timing and structure?"
2. "What would you need to see about a buyer before you'd give an LOI your time?"

His answer to the second is the list you take back to Carla.

- **Strength:** a known broker put the claim in writing on her letterhead. That is more than a phone rumor.
- **Weakness:** the letter comes from the party with a fee at stake, not from whoever holds the money. On the capital ladder this is stated interest and nothing higher.

## Do not imply

- That you have a buyer, or a buyer for his building
- That funds are verified or that this is a "cash buyer" as a fact
- An offer, a price, or a price range
- That you represent the buyer, or that you represent Rick, unless one of those is true
- That Rick is a seller

## Three things that would change this call

Each can be settled with one text to Carla before this afternoon, or by you:

1. **Has her client seen Powerline Road, or only "the area"?** If she confirms specific interest, the opening can say so.
2. **What may you repeat?** Her name, her firm, the cash claim, the ceiling. If she clears her name, "a broker I know" becomes a name Rick can check.
3. **Who do you represent here, and who pays you?** Rick will ask right after he asks about the buyer. Have a plain answer ready, and follow your state's agency disclosure rules.

Worth asking Carla at the same time: will the client release a bank proof of funds, even with the name redacted, and does she have a signed representation agreement? Confirming funds with a bank needs the client's authorization.
