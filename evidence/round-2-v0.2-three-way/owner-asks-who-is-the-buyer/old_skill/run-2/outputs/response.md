# REASON FOR MY CALL — Rick, owner, Powerline Road

**Short version:** you do not know who the buyer is, so say that. The record supports an exploratory call, not "I have a cash buyer." The honest answer is also the stronger one: Rick will respect "I don't know yet, and here is what I'd make them show you" far more than a dodge he catches later.

Audience: Rick (owner) | Purpose: learn whether he would entertain a sale if a buyer is substantiated | Stage: pre-LOI | Decider: Rick
Call type: **Exploratory.** There is no mandate, no offer and no named principal in the record.

## Color behind the call

| Claim | Tag | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Carla sent a one-page letter on her firm's letterhead | VERIFIED as a statement | You have the letter |
| Her client can pay cash up to $15M | REPORTED | Carla's letter only; nothing from a bank |
| The client is looking for a warehouse in your area | REPORTED | Carla |
| Buyer identity, who signs, who takes title | UNKNOWN | Withheld until a signed LOI |
| Carla is authorized to represent the client on a purchase | UNKNOWN | She is a tenant-rep broker; no engagement letter seen |
| Rick's building fits the requirement | UNKNOWN | "A warehouse in my area" is not "this building" |
| Rick wants to sell, and at what number | UNKNOWN | Not asked yet |

- **Strength:** you have closed deals with Carla. That is a fair reason to pick up the phone, and you can say so.
- **Weakness:** a broker's letter is a broker indication. It is not proof of funds, and it does not establish that a buyer exists, has $15M available, or can close.
- **Watch the $15M:** it is a reported ceiling on what the client can spend, not a price on Rick's building. Do not let it become the anchor.

## Opening

> "Ring ring. Rick, [your name] with [firm]. Reason for my call: a broker I've closed deals with tells me she has a client who wants to buy a warehouse in this area for cash. I haven't seen the buyer's name or anything from their bank, so I'm not calling with an offer. I'm calling to ask whether you'd want to hear more if it checks out."

## When he asks "Who's the buyer?"

> "I don't know yet, and I'm not going to pretend I do. Their broker is holding the name until there's a signed LOI. What I have is a one-page letter from her, on her firm's letterhead, saying her client can pay cash up to $15 million. That's her word, not a bank's. I've done deals with her and she's performed, which is why I'm calling instead of throwing it away. But before you spend real time on this, I'd want two things in front of you: proof of funds from the bank itself, and who actually signs and takes title. If they need an NDA to give you that, fine. If they won't give it at all, you'll have your answer."

Likely follow-ups:

- **"So is it real?"** "I can't tell you that today. I can tell you what would make it real, and I'll go get it if you're interested."
- **"Is $15 million the offer?"** "No. That's the most she says her client can spend. Nobody has put a number on your building."
- **"Who are you working for?"** Answer this one exactly as it is. If you have no agreement with Rick or with the buyer, say so, and say how you would expect to be paid. I don't know your position here, so I have not scripted it.

## Two questions worth asking

1. "If a buyer showed you bank proof of funds and told you who they are, is a sale something you'd look at, or is the building not for sale at any price right now?"
2. "What would you need to see, and from whom, before you'd sign an LOI?"

Then listen. His answer to the second question becomes your ask to Carla.

## Do not imply

- That you have a buyer, or that you have verified one.
- That the funds are confirmed, or that the buyer is "all cash, no contingencies."
- That there is an offer, or that $15M is a price for his building.
- That the buyer has seen, toured or picked his building.
- That you know why Rick would sell.

## Before you dial (three asks for Carla, if you can reach her first)

1. **May I use your name and firm with the owner?** I assumed not, so the scripts above say "a broker I've closed deals with." If she agrees, name her; it helps.
2. **Will the client give the owner bank proof of funds and the buyer's identity under an NDA before the LOI is signed?** An LOI needs a named signer anyway, so the real question is whether Rick negotiates blind until then.
3. **Does this building fit?** Size, use, timing, and whether the client would occupy it or hold it as an investment. If the client is an occupier and Rick's building is leased, that changes the conversation. Also ask whether she has a written engagement covering a purchase.

## Status

- **Examination: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.** The buyer's existence, identity and funds rest on one broker's letter.
- **Execution readiness: not examined.** There is no deal yet to test.
