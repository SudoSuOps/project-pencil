Tell Rick exactly what you have and nothing more: a broker you trust, a client you can't name, and a claim of cash you haven't verified. Don't call it a "qualified cash buyer" on the phone, because a letter on Carla's own letterhead is her word, not proof of funds.

## The opening

> "Rick, quick one. A broker I've closed deals with has a client looking to buy a warehouse in this submarket, and your building on Powerline fits what they're after. She tells me they're a cash buyer. I haven't seen anything from their bank yet, so I'm not calling to hand you an offer. I'm calling to ask one thing: if the right buyer showed up, verified, would you look at it?"

That asks him for a yes to a conversation, not a yes to a buyer you can't back up.

## When he asks "who's the buyer?"

> "I don't know yet, and I'm not going to guess. The client stays confidential until there's a signed LOI. That's their rule, and it's common enough with users who don't want the market knowing they're shopping. What I have is the broker: I've done deals with her and she's closed. What I don't have is a name or a bank letter. So here's what I'd do. Before you spend an hour on this, I get you proof of funds from their bank or their attorney, name blacked out if they want. If they can't produce that, I'll tell you and we're done. If they can, then you decide whether to look at an LOI."

If he pushes ("I don't deal with ghosts"):

> "Fair, and I wouldn't ask you to. You don't sign anything, show any financials, or let anyone walk the building until you've seen the money is real. And the LOI is non-binding, so you get the name before you're committed to anything."

## What not to say

- **Don't lead with $15M.** That's her client's stated ceiling, not a price on Rick's building. Say it and he hears "$15M offer," and you've set his number for him. If he asks about capacity, say "they tell me they can handle a building this size in cash, and I'll confirm that in writing."
- **Don't vouch for the money.** "She tells me" and "I haven't verified" keep you honest if it falls apart.
- **Don't guess at the buyer's type or identity**, even loosely.

## Before the call, if you can reach Carla

1. **Ask for third-party proof of funds:** a bank or attorney letter confirming liquid funds of at least the amount, with the name redacted. Her answer tells you how real this is.
2. **Ask whether the name can be released under an NDA at LOI submission** rather than after signing. Rick will want to know who he's signing with, and many owners won't countersign blind.
3. **Ask whether the client is buying to occupy.** She's a tenant-rep, so this is probably a user who decided to own. That matters to Rick for timing, leaseback, and any existing tenants.
4. **Settle your fee before you give either side specifics.** Get it in writing who you represent and who pays you, before Carla has the address and before Rick has her name.

If Carla won't produce the bank letter, still make the call if you want to, but shrink it to: "Would you sell, and at what number?" That is worth knowing whether or not this buyer exists.
