Tell Rick exactly what you have and nothing more: a broker you trust says she has a cash buyer, and you have not verified the buyer or the money yourself. A letter on Carla's own letterhead is her word, not proof of funds, so don't call the buyer "qualified" or "verified."

## The opening

> "Rick, it's [name]. I'm not calling to pitch you a listing. A broker I've closed deals with has a client looking to buy a warehouse in this area, all cash, and your building on Powerline fits what they want. I don't know if you'd ever sell, so the only question today is: if the number and the terms were right, is that a conversation you'd have?"

Then stop talking and let him answer. Don't lead with the $15M. It is her client's ceiling, not an offer on his building, and once you say it, it becomes his floor.

## When he asks "who's the buyer?"

> "I don't know the name yet, and I won't guess. The buyer is represented by Carla [last name] at [firm]. I've done deals with her and she's been straight with me. Her client stays confidential until there's a signed LOI, which is common with users who don't want the market knowing they're looking.
>
> Here's what I have: a letter from Carla saying her client can pay cash up to the range your building would fall in. Here's what I don't have: anything from a bank, or the buyer's name. So I'm treating this as a real lead, not a verified buyer.
>
> You don't have to take anyone's word for it. Before you sign anything binding, you get the name and proof of funds from their bank. If they won't provide it at that point, we walk and you've lost nothing but a phone call."

## If he pushes

- **"I don't deal with mystery buyers."** "Fair. I'm not asking you to deal with them yet. I'm asking whether you'd look at a written offer. An LOI is non-binding, and you'll know who they are before anything binds you."
- **"What will they pay?"** "They haven't seen your building, so there's no number on it yet. What would it take for you to sell?" If he won't name one, ask what he'd need to see to take it seriously.
- **"Is this real?"** "I believe it is, because of who's bringing it. I haven't verified it, and I'll tell you the moment I have."

## Before you dial

1. **Ask Carla for better paper.** Get a bank or attorney letter confirming funds with the buyer's name redacted, or a commitment to deliver proof of funds within a few days of a signed LOI. Also ask what she can say without the name: user or investor, industry, why this submarket, timing.
2. **Confirm with Carla that the cash is not subject to financing, a 1031 exchange, or a board or investment committee approval.** Any of those changes what you can tell Rick.
3. **Settle your fee before you give either side the other's identity.** If you don't represent Rick, ask on this call: "If I bring you an offer you accept, will you sign a one-time fee agreement for this buyer?" Get it in writing before you give Carla the address.
4. **Decide what you want from the call.** The goal is permission to bring an LOI plus the basic facts on the building (size, clear height, occupancy and lease terms, any debt), not a price negotiation.

Do not hint at who the buyer might be, even as speculation. If you're wrong, it costs you Rick's trust. If you're right, it costs you Carla's.
