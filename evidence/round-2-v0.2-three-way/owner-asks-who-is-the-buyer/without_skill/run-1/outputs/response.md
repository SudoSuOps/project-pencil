Tell Rick the truth: you don't know who the buyer is, and here is exactly what you do have. That answer is stronger than it sounds, because what you're asking him for is small.

**What you actually have**

- A broker you've closed deals with says she has a client who can pay cash up to $15M.
- Her letter, on her own letterhead, says the same thing.
- That is a broker's representation. It is not proof of funds, and you have not verified the buyer or the money.

So don't say "I have a cash buyer," "a qualified buyer," or "proof of funds." Say "a broker I trust tells me she has one."

**The opening**

> "Rick, it's [name]. Quick one, and I'll be straight about what I know and what I don't. A broker I've closed deals with has a client looking for a warehouse in this area, and she says they can pay cash up to $15 million. Powerline Road fits what they're after. I'm not calling with an offer. I'm calling to ask whether you'd look at one if it came in at a number that made sense."

**When he asks who the buyer is**

> "I don't know, and I'm not going to guess. The buyer's broker is keeping the name confidential until there's a signed LOI. What I have is her word and a one-page letter from her firm saying the client can pay cash up to $15 million. Nothing from a bank yet. I've done deals with her and she's been straight with me, which is why I'm calling you at all, but I haven't verified the money myself."

Then go straight to what protects him:

> "So I'm not asking you to take anything on faith. All I want to know today is whether you'd entertain an offer. If the answer is yes, you get the buyer's name and proof of funds from their bank before you sign anything binding or let anyone through the building. If they won't provide that, it stops there and you've lost a phone call."

**If he pushes**

- *"I don't deal with mystery buyers."* "Fair. Would you look at a written LOI if it came with a bank letter, even with the name held back until you countersign? I'll go back and ask for that."
- *"Is it a user or an investor?"* Only answer if Carla has told you. Otherwise: "I don't know yet. I'll ask her and call you back."
- *"What's the number?"* "Their ceiling is $15 million. That's not an offer. What would it take for you to pay attention?" Then let him talk.
- *"Who do you represent?"* Answer this plainly, and work out your answer before you dial. Whether you're working for him, cooperating with Carla, or acting as an intermediary, he should hear it from you on this call, along with how you expect to be paid.

**Two calls to Carla before you call Rick**

1. Ask what she can release now without naming the client: a bank or attorney letter with the name redacted, whether the buyer is an owner-user or an investor, and whether the purchase depends on financing or the sale of another property. Any one of these gives you something better than her letterhead.
2. Ask whether the client would sign an NDA with Rick so the name can be disclosed earlier, and whether "confidential until signed LOI" means the name appears in the LOI or only after it is countersigned.

If she gives you nothing more, the script above still works. You just say "that's all I have today."

**One thing to settle on this call or right after:** if Rick is open to it, get your fee arrangement with him in writing before any name or offer changes hands.
