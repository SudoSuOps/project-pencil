**Assessment: capacity is established, subject to four confirmations.** The broker's concern is about track record, which is a different question from capacity and is best handled through deal terms.

## What you have

Capacity comes down to two things: can the buyer pay, and can the person signing bind the buyer.

- **Funds:** $9M against a $7.2M all-cash price is 1.25x coverage, a $1.8M cushion. That comfortably absorbs closing costs, prorations and reserves. Counsel authenticating the letter directly with the bank is the strongest form of proof of funds short of money in escrow.
- **Authority:** a signed resolution naming the managing member as authorized signatory covers the signing question on its face.
- **No financing risk:** all cash removes the lender, appraisal and loan-approval contingencies, which are where most buyers fail to close.

## What to confirm

1. **Whose account it is.** The LLC is a month old, so the $9M may sit in a principal's or affiliate's name. If so, the LLC itself has no money yet. Get a funding commitment from the account holder, or see the funds moved into the LLC.
2. **What "available" means.** It should be unrestricted cash on deposit, not a credit line, a pledged account or securities that need to be sold.
3. **The date of the letter.** A letter shows a balance on one day. Require a refreshed one at the end of due diligence or just before closing.
4. **The resolution against the operating agreement.** Check that whoever signed the resolution had the power to adopt it, that no member consent threshold applies to a purchase this size, and that the LLC is in good standing. Title will ask for the same documents.

Escrow and title will also run their own identity and source-of-funds checks on the principals, so have that information ready.

## The broker's concern

A newly formed LLC is normal. Most commercial buyers take title in a new single-purpose entity, so the entity having no track record says very little. The real issue is that the people behind it are first-time buyers, and that is a performance risk rather than a capacity risk: retrading, slow due diligence or cold feet.

The seller's remedy on a buyer default is usually limited to the deposit, so that is where to address it:

- **Larger earnest money deposit**, for example 3-5% rather than 1-2%.
- **Deposit goes hard early**, with some or all of it non-refundable at the end of a short due diligence period.
- **Short timeline**: 21-30 days of due diligence and a 15-30 day close.
- **Disclose the principals** and where their money comes from, so the broker is evaluating people rather than a shell.
- **Experienced team**: name the buyer's counsel and due diligence consultants to offset the inexperience.

## Bottom line

On the facts given, the buyer can pay and the signatory can bind it, once the four items above check out. A first-time buyer with verified cash and a meaningful hard deposit is a lower closing risk than an experienced buyer who needs a loan. I would present it to the seller's broker in those terms.
