## Answering the seller's broker

The broker's concern is about performance risk. Track record is one proxy for that; authenticated money and authority are more direct evidence. Options that speak to the concern, for the buyer to decide on:

- Share the bank letter (redacted as needed) and the resolution with the seller's side, or have counsel confirm to seller's counsel directly. Sharing bank information, and any confirmation by the seller's side with the bank, needs the buyer's authorization.
- Put more of the commitment into the contract: a larger earnest money deposit, an earlier date for it to go nonrefundable, or a shorter diligence period. How any of these operate is for counsel to draft and confirm.
- Offer a refreshed bank letter at a set point before closing.
- State the short history plainly. "New entity, first CRE purchase, funds and authority documented" is defendable. Leaving it out is what creates a credibility problem later.

Do not describe the buyer to the seller's side as "proven" or "experienced". "Authenticated funds of $9M against a $7.2M price, documented signing authority, no prior closings" is what the record supports.
