## What is still open

1. **Account holder.** If the $9M sits in the LLC's own account, funds and title-taking entity line up. If it sits with a member or an affiliate, the LLC needs a documented path to that money, such as a capital commitment or funding it into the LLC or escrow.
2. **Letter date and limits.** A letter shows a balance on a date. Confirm the date, and whether the funds are unrestricted and uncommitted elsewhere.
3. **Resolution against the operating agreement.** Confirm who signed the resolution and that the operating agreement gives them the power to grant this authority, with no other member consent required. This is counsel's call.

Also unknown: closing costs and prorations (the surplus looks ample, but no figure was supplied), and whether the LLC is the entity taking title.

## On the seller's broker's concern

The concern is about history. The question for this deal is present ability to close, and that is answered by authenticated funds and documented authority, not by résumé length. A long track record would not have substituted for either.

What a new entity genuinely cannot show is how it behaves under a contract. The usual answer is deal structure rather than more diligence on the buyer: deposit size, when it goes hard, and the length of the diligence and closing periods. Those are for the seller and counsel to set.

Sharing the bank letter or the fact of its authentication with the seller's broker needs the buyer's authorization and should stay inside your disclosure scope.
