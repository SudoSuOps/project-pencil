**Diligence expires April 30. Closing is still May 18, unless the PSA defines closing by reference to the diligence period, which you need to check.** The broker's timeline is stale and should be corrected.

## Why

- **Diligence: April 30.** A written amendment signed by both parties controls over the original PSA on the point it addresses. It was signed April 10, before the original April 16 expiration, so there is no lapse-and-revival problem.
- **Closing: May 18, on the facts you gave.** An amendment that is silent on closing does not move it. Most amendments also say that, except as modified, the PSA remains in full force.

## The one thing that could change the closing date

Read how the PSA states the closing date. It matters whether it is a fixed date or a formula.

- **Fixed date ("Closing shall occur on May 18"):** closing stays May 18. You now have 18 days between diligence expiry and closing instead of 32.
- **Formula ("Closing shall occur 30 days after expiration of the Due Diligence Period"):** extending diligence moves closing automatically, even though the amendment never mentions it.

The formula reading is plausible here. April 16 plus 30 days is May 16. If this is 2026, May 16 is a Saturday, and a standard business-day rollover lands on Monday, May 18. On that reading, the new closing would be 30 days after April 30, which is Saturday, May 30, rolling to Monday, June 1.

## Other dates to re-check

These often key off the diligence expiration and may have moved with it:

- **Earnest money going hard**, and any additional deposit due at or after diligence expiry.
- **Title and survey objection and response deadlines.**
- **Financing contingency**, if there is one. Check whether it runs from the effective date or from diligence expiry.
- **Termination notice mechanics.** Confirm the cutoff time and time zone on April 30 (often 5:00 p.m. local) and the required delivery method.
- **Any extension fee or added deposit** the amendment required as a condition of the extension. Confirm it was paid.

3. If closing is fixed at May 18 and 18 days is too tight for the lender or title, ask for a second amendment now rather than in mid-May.
4. If the closing date is at all ambiguous, get a one-line confirmation signed by both sides ("Closing Date remains May 18" or "Closing Date is June 1").
