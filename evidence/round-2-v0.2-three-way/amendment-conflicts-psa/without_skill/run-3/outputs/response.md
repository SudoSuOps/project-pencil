## Closing: depends on how the PSA words it

The amendment's silence means the closing provision is unchanged. What "unchanged" produces depends on the PSA's wording, which I have not seen.

| How the PSA states closing | Result |
|---|---|
| Fixed date ("Closing shall occur on May 18") | Closing stays May 18. The gap between diligence expiry and closing shrinks from 32 days to 18. |
| Formula ("Closing shall occur 30 days after expiration of the Due Diligence Period") | Closing moves with diligence: April 30 + 30 days = May 30, a Saturday, which would typically roll to Monday, June 1. |

**Your dates look like formula dates, so check this before assuming May 18.** This assumes the year is 2026:

- March 2 + 45 days = April 16, which is a standard 45-day diligence period.
- April 16 + 30 days = May 16, a Saturday, and the next business day is Monday, May 18.

That pattern suggests the PSA says "45 days after the Effective Date" and "30 days after expiration of the Due Diligence Period", and that March 2, April 16 and May 18 are someone's calculated summary. If so, the extension pulled closing out to about June 1, whether or not the parties intended that.

## What to read

1. **PSA closing-date definition.** Fixed date or keyed to diligence expiry?
2. **PSA definition of "Due Diligence Period".** Check for a time-of-day cutoff (often 5:00 p.m. in a stated time zone) and a business-day rollover clause.
3. **The amendment's exact wording.** "Extended to April 30" is a fixed date; "extended by 14 days" is not. Also check for the usual "except as modified, the PSA remains in full force" clause and for any statement about closing.
4. **Every other deadline keyed to diligence expiry.** These move to April 30 too unless the amendment says otherwise:
   - earnest money going hard and any additional deposit
   - title and survey objection and cure periods
   - the financing contingency, if separate
   - estoppel and SNDA delivery
   - the termination notice deadline and the notice method
   - any closing-extension option and its notice deadline

## What to do

- **Correct the broker's timeline now**, and make sure escrow/title and the lender have the amendment.
- **If the PSA uses a formula, or is at all ambiguous, get the closing date confirmed in writing.** A one-paragraph second amendment stating the date removes the argument. Without it, each side can later claim whichever date suits them.
- **If closing really is fixed at May 18,** tell the lender and title company that the post-diligence window is now 18 days and confirm they can still close.

This is a reading of the facts as you gave them, not of the documents. Have deal counsel confirm against the actual PSA and amendment before anyone relies on a date, especially for deposit-hard and termination-notice purposes.
