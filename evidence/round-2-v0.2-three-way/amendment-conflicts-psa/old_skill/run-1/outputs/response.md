## The three dates

**Diligence: April 30.** The later amendment, signed by both parties, conflicts with the original April 16. The later signed date is the one that appears to control. It was signed April 10, six days before the original expiry, so on these facts there is no lapse-then-revive question.

**Closing: May 18, with one check.** An amendment that is silent on closing normally leaves the PSA's closing term as written. What matters is how the PSA states it:

- If it is a fixed date ("May 18"), it appears unchanged.
- If it is a formula ("X days after expiration of the Due Diligence Period"), moving diligence may move closing with it, and May 18 would just be someone's arithmetic off the old date.

Read the closing section and the amendment's "except as modified, the PSA remains in full force" language, then have counsel confirm.

**Broker's timeline: April 16 is stale.** A broker's timeline is a working document, not a contract document. It doesn't change the clock, but it does cause people to act on the wrong date. Reissue it citing the amendment.

## What the amendment changes without saying so

- **The back end got shorter.** The gap from diligence expiry to closing drops from 32 days to 18 (April 16 to May 18 vs. April 30 to May 18). Lender, title, survey, and closing deliverables now have 14 fewer days after the deposit is at risk. If the buyer is financing, that is the gap to test.
- **Dates keyed to diligence expiry may have moved too.** Deposit going hard, any additional deposit, termination notice, and title or survey objection deadlines are often tied to "expiration of the Due Diligence Period." Whether they moved to April 30 or stayed on fixed dates depends on the PSA's wording.
- **Day-count is open.** I don't know how the PSA counts days or what time of day a deadline falls. No year was stated either, so I haven't put weekdays on any of this.
