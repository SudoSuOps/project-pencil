## What the amendment changes in practice

- **Diligence is 14 days longer.** It was 45 days from execution (March 2 to April 16) and is now 59 (March 2 to April 30).
- **The run from diligence expiry to closing shrinks from 32 days to 18** (April 30 to May 18). Everything that normally happens after diligence (lender conditions, title and survey clearance, closing documents, funding) now has 14 fewer days if closing really stays at May 18.
- **The amendment was signed 6 days before the original expiry**, so on your facts it was in place before April 16 arrived.

## What silence on closing does not settle

These are the points that could move the answer. Each one needs the actual text.

1. **How the PSA defines closing.** If closing is a fixed date (May 18), silence most likely leaves it there. If it is defined as a number of days after diligence expires, extending diligence could push closing out with it. Thirty-two days after April 30 would be June 1. I cannot tell which from your description.
2. **Other dates keyed to diligence expiry.** The deposit going hard, any additional deposit, title and survey objection deadlines, a financing contingency, and extension notice windows are often tied to the diligence date. They may have moved to April 30 too, or they may be fixed dates that did not move.
3. **What exactly the amendment extended.** "Diligence" could mean the whole period or only a named item, and the amendment may or may not say that all other PSA terms stay in force.
4. **Day counting.** Whether the PSA counts calendar or business days, and what happens when a deadline lands on a weekend or holiday, is an open item.
