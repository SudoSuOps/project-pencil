## Why even the $600K is probably high

- **They are the buyer's numbers.** One bid from the buyer's contractor is an opening position. Get your own bids for both items. Check whether the roof bid is a full tear-off when a recover or coating would do, and whether the HVAC figure is replacement of everything or just the units at end of life.
- **Replacement cost is not the same as lost value.** The buyer was never buying a new roof. If the roof has 5 years left on a 20-year life, the fair credit is the used-up portion or the present value of pulling the spend forward, not 100% of a new one. Full credit hands them a brand-new roof at your expense.
- **Who actually pays?** If leases are NNN or tenants are responsible for HVAC maintenance and replacement, or roof cost can be amortized through CAM, much of this cost never lands on the owner. Check the lease language before conceding a dollar.
- **Was it already priced in?** If the roof and HVAC age were disclosed in the OM, visible on the tour, or reflected in the agreed price or cap rate, this is a retrade on known conditions rather than a diligence discovery.

## What could justify the other $1.9M

Nothing you've described. Legitimate reasons would look like: environmental findings, structural issues, a rent roll or estoppel that came in below what was represented, a tenant giving notice, an appraisal shortfall, or a move in their debt terms. Each of those comes with a document. If the buyer has one, they should produce it. If the real reason is financing or cold feet, that is a negotiation about price, not about condition, and should be treated as one.

## How I'd respond

1. **Ask for an itemized breakdown of the full $2.5M** with supporting reports. Do not counter until you have it. Countering a number with no backup validates it.
2. **Get your own roof and HVAC bids** in the meantime, and pull the lease clauses on roof and HVAC responsibility.
3. **Frame your counter around documented, owner-borne, unanticipated cost.** Depending on remaining useful life and lease structure, that realistically lands somewhere between zero and a fraction of $600K, well short of the full amount.
4. **Consider a repair credit or escrow holdback instead of a price cut**, or doing the work yourself before closing. That caps your exposure at actual cost.
5. **Check your leverage before you set the tone.** Where is the earnest money, has it gone hard, when does the diligence period expire, and do you have a backup buyer? If they can walk free and you have no backup, you have less room to hold the line. If the deposit is hard or a backup is waiting, you can simply say no.

## Bottom line

The defensible discussion is about some portion of $600K. The $2.5M is a retrade until they show otherwise.
