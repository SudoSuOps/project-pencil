**No, not on what you've shown me.** The two documents total $600K ($410K roof + $190K HVAC). That leaves $1.9M of the $2.5M ask, about 76%, with nothing behind it.

**The $600K is a ceiling, not a floor.** Even the documented part is open to challenge:

- **Whose numbers are these?** A bid commissioned by the buyer is priced to support a retrade. Get your own roofer and mechanical contractor to price the same scope.
- **Replacement or repair?** A full replacement bid on a roof with five years left is not a $410K problem today. The fair credit is the cost to cure what is actually deficient, or a share that reflects the remaining life.
- **Was it already in the price?** If the age and condition of the roof and HVAC were disclosed, visible on a tour, or reflected in the asking price, the buyer is asking to be paid twice.
- **Who bears the cost under the leases?** If tenants maintain or replace the HVAC, or the cost can be recovered through reimbursements, the buyer's real exposure is smaller than the estimate.
- **Betterment.** A new roof and new units leave the buyer with a better asset than the one they bid on. A full dollar-for-dollar credit overpays them.

A defensible number for these two items is probably somewhere between $300K and $600K, depending on how those questions come out.

**What the other $1.9M might be.** There are only a few possibilities:

1. **Undisclosed diligence findings**, such as environmental, structural, paving, code or lease issues. If they exist, the buyer should produce the reports.
2. **Capitalizing the capex.** Dividing a repair cost by the cap rate is not legitimate, because a one-time cost comes off the price once.
3. **A changed underwriting view**, such as higher debt costs, lower rent assumptions, or pushback from their lender or investment committee. That is their problem, not a property condition.
4. **An anchor.** They may be asking for $2.5M in the hope of settling at $1.2M and calling it a compromise.

**How I'd respond:**

- **Ask for an itemized schedule.** Request every dollar of the $2.5M, line by line, with the supporting report or bid. Don't counter until you have it, because a counter against a lump sum just splits their anchor.
- **Get your own bids** on the roof and HVAC so you are negotiating from your numbers.
- **Counter only on what's documented**, as a closing credit, a seller repair before closing, or an escrow holdback. A credit or holdback usually costs you less than a price reduction of the same size.
- **Check your leverage first.** It matters whether their deposit has gone hard, whether the diligence period has expired, whether you have a backup buyer, and how much a failed deal and relisting would cost you.

Three things would let me give you a tighter number: the purchase price, the property type and lease structure, and whether the buyer gave any explanation for the $2.5M beyond those two documents.
