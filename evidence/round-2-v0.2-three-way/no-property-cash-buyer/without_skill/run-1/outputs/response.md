Right now you have a lead on a buyer, not a buyer. Dale is an intermediary with an unnamed principal and no proof of funds, so the owner call should be a market-check call in your own name, not a "I have a cash buyer" call. If you pitch it as a live buyer and Dale evaporates, it costs you with an owner you know.

## What you actually have

- **Known:** Dale says he represents a cash buyer for South Florida industrial, $10–20M.
- **Not known:** who the buyer is, whether the money exists, whether Dale has a direct relationship or is one of several people in a chain, and what the buyer wants (county, size, vacant or leased, yield).
- **Read:** "cash, $10–20M, industrial, South Florida" with no other criteria is the most common unqualified pitch there is. It can be real, but a real buyer's rep can normally give you a buy box in two minutes.

## Qualify Dale before you spend the owner relationship

Call him back and ask:

1. Who is the buyer, or at least what kind: owner-user, 1031 exchange, family office, fund?
2. Are you direct to the principal, and do you have a signed buyer-rep agreement?
3. What have they closed in the last 24 months, and where?
4. What is the buy box: county and submarket, size, clear height, vacant or leased, lease term, target cap rate?
5. If 1031: when did the relinquished property close? That sets the 45-day and 180-day clocks.
6. Will they sign an NDA and send proof of funds (bank letter or statement with the name visible) before getting an address?
7. How is Dale getting paid, and by whom?

Check his license on the Florida DBPR site. Get your fee agreement or buyer registration in writing before you name any property, assuming your own license is active and placed with a brokerage.

If he can't answer 1 through 4, treat him as a reason to call the owner and nothing more.

What that means for this call:

- **Size band:** at $268–$284/SF, $10–20M buys roughly 35,000–75,000 SF. That is my arithmetic from market averages; small-bay and newer product trade above them. This is private-owner and owner-user territory, which is why a quiet call can work.
- **Income band:** at about a 5.5% cap, $10–20M implies roughly $550K–$1.1M of NOI.
- **Pricing is holding while leasing is soft.** Miami-Dade price per SF is at a record even with vacancy up, and the trailing-12-month sales volume there is $2.8B, down 4% (Matthews calls its volume figures directional). That is a fair reason to tell an owner it's a reasonable moment to hear a number.
- **Supply is thinning.** The regional pipeline is about half of last year's, which helps existing buildings.
- **Broward is the soft spot.** Vacancy rose to 6.9% on three buildings delivered empty, and CBRE reports more than 3M SF of occupied listed space coming available, about 2M of it in Q4. If the owner's building is in Broward with near-term rollover, expect the buyer to price that risk.

I did not pull Palm Beach County figures or submarket comps; get those for the owner's specific building before quoting him a number.

## Opening for the owner call

> "[Name], it's Smash. Quick one, and I'll tell you up front I'm not calling with an offer. I got a call this morning from a broker who says he has a cash buyer for South Florida industrial in the ten-to-twenty range. I haven't verified the buyer yet and I'm not going to give anyone your address until I do. Your building came to mind because it fits the size. Before I do any work on it: is there a number where you'd want me to bring you something real, or is it a hold no matter what?"

Then stop talking. Depending on the answer:

- **"What's the number?"** "At market averages, buildings your size are trading around $270–$285 a foot and mid-5 caps. Yours depends on the rent roll. Tell me your lease expirations and I'll give you a tighter range."
- **"Not selling."** "Understood. What would have to change: price, a tax solution, a leaseback?" Note the answer and the date.
- **"Who's the buyer?"** "I don't know yet, and that's why I'm not asking you for anything today. If they prove up, I'll come back with a name and proof of funds first."

Get three things from the call: his price or his no, his lease rollover dates, and permission to come back with a verified buyer. Do not send Dale the address, the owner's name, or the rent roll until you have proof of funds, an NDA, and your fee in writing.
