"South Florida industrial, $10–20M, all cash" is also the most generic buy box in the market. Real buyers usually narrow it fast: submarket, size, clear height, vacant versus leased, timing, 1031 or not.

## Call Dale back first

Ten minutes with Dale makes the owner call much stronger. Ask him:

1. **Who is the buyer?** If he won't name them, ask for the type (user, private investor, fund, 1031) and what they have closed recently.
2. **Is he engaged in writing?** Is it exclusive, and who pays his fee?
3. **Is he licensed in Florida?** Check him on the DBPR site either way.
4. **What exactly do they want?** Counties, building size, user or investor, vacancy tolerance, yard or outdoor storage, cap rate or price-per-foot ceiling.
5. **Why now?** A 1031 clock, a lease expiration or a fund deadline is real urgency. "Just looking" is not.
6. **Will they show funds?** Ask for a proof-of-funds letter or a banker reference to you, under an NDA if needed, before any property is identified.

If he ducks most of these, he is probably a wholesaler or a broker fishing for inventory to shop around. If he answers cleanly, you have something worth taking to an owner.

Protect yourself before naming any property or owner: get a registration or co-brokerage agreement in writing, and a fee agreement with the owner.

## Opening for the owner call

Your credibility with this owner is worth more than this lead, so lead with the honest version. It still works, because you are asking for a conversation, not a commitment.

> "Hey [Name], it's Smash. Quick one, and I'll be straight about what I know and don't. I got a call this morning from a broker who says he has a cash buyer for South Florida industrial in the $10 to $20 million range. I haven't verified the buyer or the money yet, so I'm not bringing you an offer. Your building is the first one I thought of. Before I spend time vetting this guy, I wanted to ask: if a real, qualified buyer showed up at the right number, is that a conversation you'd want to have, or is it not for sale at any price right now?"

Then stop talking. Follow-ups, depending on the answer:

- **If he's open:** "What would the number need to start with for you to take it seriously?" and "Anything on timing or taxes that would shape a deal, like a 1031 or a leaseback?"
- **If he's lukewarm:** "Fair enough. If I confirm the buyer is real and it fits your building, do you want me to call you, or leave you alone?"
- **If it's a no:** "Understood. What would have to change for that answer to be different?" Then move on to how the building is doing.

## What not to say

- Don't say "I have a cash buyer." You have a caller who says he has one.
- Don't quote a price or imply an offer is coming.
- Don't give Dale the owner's name or address until he has shown you the buyer is real and your fee is protected.
