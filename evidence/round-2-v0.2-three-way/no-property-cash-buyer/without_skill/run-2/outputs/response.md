Short version: right now you have a phone call, not a buyer. That is fine for opening a conversation with an owner you already know, as long as you describe it to him as exactly that. Below is where Dale stands, the market color you can use, and an opening that does not overstate what you have.

## Where Dale stands

What you actually know: a man named Dale called, said he reps a cash buyer, said industrial, South Florida, $10-20M. Everything else is unverified: no proof of funds, no buyer name, no criteria beyond a price band.

"Cash, $10-20M, industrial, South Florida" is the loosest buy box there is. Real buyers at that size usually come with a submarket, a size range, a product type, and a reason (1031 clock, user requirement, fund mandate). The vagueness does not prove Dale is a daisy-chain wholesaler, but it is what one sounds like.

Before you spend the owner relationship on him, get these from Dale:

1. **His license and firm.** Look him up on the Florida DBPR site. Takes two minutes.
2. **His relationship to the buyer.** Direct, with a signed buyer rep agreement, or is he one step removed from someone who knows someone?
3. **The buyer's identity, or a reason for withholding it.** Offer to sign an NDA or a registration letter. A real rep can work with that.
4. **Proof of funds or a closed-deal list.** A bank or brokerage statement, or two or three recent closings you can verify in public records.
5. **A real buy box.** Counties, size, clear height, single vs. multi-tenant, vacant/user vs. leased investment, cap rate floor, 1031 or not, timing.
6. **Fees in writing.** Who pays you, who pays him, before any address changes hands.

If he balks at all six, you have your answer and it cost you one call.

## Market color (Q3 2026)

Two sources, slightly different methodologies, same direction:

- **Vacancy has turned.** Lee & Associates puts South Florida industrial at 6.5%, down from 6.7% the prior quarter. Colliers, as reported by Florida Realtors, runs higher by metro: Miami 7.74%, Fort Lauderdale 7.47%, Palm Beach 8.45%, all above the 7.23% national rate.
- **Absorption is positive again.** Lee shows about +527K SF over the trailing 12 months versus roughly -3.5M SF a year earlier. Colliers has Miami alone at about +1.4M SF in Q3, with Fort Lauderdale near +180K and Palm Beach near +86K.
- **Rents are still inching up.** About $17.82/SF NNN regionally (Lee), up from $17.29 a year ago; Colliers has Miami at $17.72.
- **The pipeline has been cut in half.** About 3.8M SF under construction, down 52% year over year.
- **Pricing.** Lee cites an average sale price of about $268/SF. At that average, $10-20M is roughly 37,000 to 75,000 SF, which is the private-owner, small-bay and mid-size single-tenant end of the market, not institutional big box. For scale, Ares paid $65.3M for a Hialeah property this quarter.

How to read it for an owner: the softening of the last two years looks like it has bottomed, new supply is thinning, and rent growth is modest, not 2022-style. An owner who has been waiting for the market to "come back" has a reasonable case that the window is reopening. An owner with below-market leases rolling has a reason to hold.

What I do not have: a sourced cap rate for this size band this quarter. Neither report gave one. Use your own recent comps for that, and do not let me or Dale put a number in the owner's head.

Sources: [Lee & Associates South Florida Q3 2026](https://www.lee-associates.com/southflorida/?p=10789); [Florida Realtors, Oct. 28, 2026, citing Colliers](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/10/florida-warehouse-market-shows-uneven-third-quarter-gains).

## The opening

Fill in the brackets. It leads with the relationship and the market, mentions the inquiry honestly, and asks a question he can answer without committing to anything.

> "[Name], it's [you]. Got two minutes? I'm calling because I had an inbound this morning from a broker who says he has a cash buyer for South Florida industrial in the ten to twenty range. I'll be straight with you: I haven't verified the buyer yet and I'm not going to bring you anyone until I have. But it made me think of [property], and the market's shifted enough this year that I wanted to ask. Vacancy's ticking down, construction's off by half, and prices in your size range are holding. If the right number showed up, is that a conversation you'd want to have, or is it a hold for you right now?"

Then stop talking.

Follow-ups depending on what he says:

- **"What's the number?"** "I don't have one from this buyer, and I wouldn't trust one yet. Let me show you what's actually closed near you in the last six months, and you tell me what would move you."
- **"Who's the buyer?"** "I don't know yet. That's the first thing I'm running down. If I can't verify him, you'll never hear about him again, but I'd still like to know where your head is."
- **"Not selling."** "Understood. What would have to change? Lease roll, a number, a tax situation?" You leave with his real trigger, which is worth more than Dale.

## Two cautions

- **Do not tell the owner you "have a cash buyer."** You do not, and if Dale evaporates, you are the one who burned the credibility. "A broker tells me he has one, unverified" is true and still gets the conversation.
- **Do not give Dale the owner's name or the address** until he is qualified and fees are in writing. An unnamed-buyer rep with an address in hand can go around you.

The real value of this call is the reason to phone the owner. If he is a seller at a number, you have a listing conversation and a market full of buyers you can verify. Dale is optional.
