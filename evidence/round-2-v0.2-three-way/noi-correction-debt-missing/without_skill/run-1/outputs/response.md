**Short version:** the $120K correction alone supports about **$22.15M**, so $22M is roughly the right number at the same cap rate. But I would not send a number yet, because the fee is probably not the only thing wrong with that T-12.

I'm reading "we" as the buy side, and working only from the figures in your message.

## What the correction does

| | NOI | Price | Cap rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original pencil | $1,560,000 | $24,000,000 | 6.50% |
| Corrected NOI, same price | $1,440,000 | $24,000,000 | 6.00% |
| Corrected NOI, same cap | $1,440,000 | $22,153,846 | 6.50% |
| Analyst's number | $1,440,000 | $22,000,000 | 6.55% |

- Pulling out the fee cuts NOI by 7.7%, which is $1,846,154 of value at a 6.50% cap.
- Holding $24.0M means paying a 6.00% cap on recurring income, a 50 bp tighter deal than the one you agreed to.
- $22M is about $154K below the straight math. That is defensible as a round number, but it is a small ask beyond the correction, not a pure re-trade to the same cap.

## The bigger issue: the tenant who left

A termination fee means a tenant is gone. That tenant's rent and reimbursements are almost certainly still in the T-12 for the months before March, and none of that is recurring either unless the space has been re-leased.

- Every $10K of that tenant's T-12 income that isn't replaced is another **$154K** of value at a 6.50% cap.
- If the suite is still vacant, the buyer also carries downtime, tenant improvements and leasing commissions to get it back.
- If it has been backfilled, the new lease replaces the old income and the adjustment could be small, or even positive.

So $1,440,000 is a ceiling on in-place NOI until we see the rent roll, not the answer.

## Debt

There is no lender quote, so I can't tell you coverage, proceeds or leveraged return, and I'd rather not invent terms. Two things are safe to say:

- The lender will size on corrected NOI, and will likely underwrite the vacancy too. Proceeds fall with NOI regardless of what price you agree.
- For illustration only: a loan sized to a 10% debt yield drops from $15.6M to $14.4M. At an unchanged $24.0M price, that $1.2M gap is extra equity.

Make sure the lender has the corrected NOI before the quote comes back, or the quote will be wrong.

## What I'd do

1. **Ask the seller for three things today:** the current rent roll, the departed tenant's lease and monthly billing history, and the status of that suite (vacant, in negotiation or re-leased).
2. **Rebuild NOI from the rent roll** rather than adjusting the T-12 by one line.
3. **Then go back with one number.** If the suite is backfilled at similar rent, $22.0M to $22.15M is the right range. If it is vacant, the number is below $22M and you should say why in the same letter.

Going back at $22M now and then again after the rent roll would mean two re-trades, which costs more credibility than waiting a few days for one.
