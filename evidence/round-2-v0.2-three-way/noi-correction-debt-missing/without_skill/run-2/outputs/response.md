## The bigger issue: the tenant that paid the fee is gone

Removing the fee only fixes half of the T-12. Two things still need checking:

1. **The departed tenant's rent is probably still in the T-12.** If they moved out in March, the trailing 12 months likely include several months of their base rent and reimbursements. That income is not recurring either, unless the space has been re-leased.
2. **The space itself.** If it is vacant, in-place NOI is below $1.44M and you are also carrying downtime, tenant improvements and leasing commissions. If it has been backfilled, the new lease's rent, start date and free rent replace the old tenant's numbers.

Every $10K of annual NOI is worth about $154K of price at a 6.5% cap. For illustration only: if that tenant paid $150K a year and the space is dark, in-place NOI would be closer to $1.29M and the price at a 6.5% cap closer to $19.8M, before lease-up costs. I don't have their rent, so treat that as the shape of the risk, not a number.
