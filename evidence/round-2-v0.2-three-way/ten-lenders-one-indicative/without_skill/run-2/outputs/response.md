**Short answer: financing is not lined up. You have one soft quote and nine lenders who have not said yes.** "Lined up" would mean a signed term sheet at minimum, and really a credit-approved commitment. You are two or three steps short of that.

## What you actually have

- **One indicative quote.** Indicative terms are a lender's first look, usually from an originator, before credit committee, appraisal, third-party reports or full underwriting. They are non-binding and are routinely re-traded on proceeds, rate or structure, or withdrawn.
- **A 10% response rate.** How bad this is depends on timing, which I don't know:
  - If the package went out in the last week or so, nine may simply be pending and this is early.
  - If it has been out three or more weeks, silence is mostly passes. That means the market is telling you something about the asset, the sponsor, the leverage or the price.
- **No competitive tension.** With a single bidder, the lender has no reason to hold its terms, and you have no fallback if it drops out.
- **An incomplete quote.** 65% / 6.4% / 5-year leaves out most of what determines whether the loan works.

## What the quote doesn't tell you

- **65% of what?** Lenders size to the lower of purchase price and appraised value, and then to DSCR and debt yield. If the appraisal comes in light or the loan is DSCR-constrained, actual proceeds can land well under 65% of price.
- **Is 6.4% fixed or floating, and is it locked?** Most indicative rates are a spread over an index that floats until rate lock. The coupon can move between now and closing.
- **Amortization and interest-only.** At 6.4% on a 30-year schedule the loan constant is about 7.5%; interest-only it is 6.4%. That difference decides whether coverage clears.
- **Recourse, reserves, prepayment, fees, extension options, and any holdbacks or earn-outs** that cut day-one proceeds.
- **Conditions and timing.** What the lender needs to see, and whether it can close inside the contract period.

## The quick test

Take the property's in-place NOI and check it against the quote:

- At 65% LTV and 6.4% interest-only, a 1.25x DSCR needs a debt yield of about 8.0%, which means a going-in cap rate of roughly 5.2% or higher on the purchase price.
- On 30-year amortization, 1.25x needs a debt yield of about 9.4%, or a cap rate of roughly 6.1% or higher.

If the deal is priced tighter than that, 65% is unlikely to survive underwriting, and the equity requirement goes up.

## The real exposure: what the buyer is telling the seller

This is the part I would deal with first.

- **If you are the buyer or on the buy side:** telling the seller financing is "lined up" overstates the position. If the seller relies on it (grants an extension, passes on a backup offer, lets a contingency lapse) and the loan falls through, you have a credibility problem and possibly a misrepresentation claim. The accurate statement is "we have indicative terms from one lender and are working toward a term sheet."
- **If you are the seller or on the sell side:** treat the claim as unverified. Ask for the written quote or term sheet, the lender's name, and a timeline to commitment. Then check what the contract says: whether there is a financing contingency, when it expires, and when the deposit goes hard.

Either way, the contract matters more than the quote. If the deposit is hard and there is no financing contingency, the buyer is carrying closing risk on one non-binding indication.

## What to do this week

1. **Get the status of the other nine from the broker**, lender by lender: passed, pending, or never responded, and the stated reason for each pass. The pass reasons are the most useful information you can get right now.
2. **Get the one quote in writing** with full terms: proceeds, sizing tests, index and spread, amortization, recourse, reserves, fees, prepayment.
3. **Push that lender to a signed term sheet** and get a date for credit committee.
4. **Size the equity for a lower-proceeds case** (say 55% to 60%) and confirm the buyer can cover the gap.
5. **Map the lender's timeline against contract dates:** contingency expiry, hard-deposit date, closing date. Appraisal and third-party reports typically take several weeks from application.
6. **Correct what is being said to the seller** so it matches what exists on paper.
7. **Keep the broker working the field.** If the pass reasons point to leverage or price, consider re-approaching at lower leverage, or widening to debt funds or the seller for a gap piece.
