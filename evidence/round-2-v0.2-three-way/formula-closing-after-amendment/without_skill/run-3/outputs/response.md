**Short answer: on the language you quoted, the contract closing date is now September 4, not August 15. The title company's calendar is stale, and the lender's August 18 is early, not late.**

## The math

| Event | Original PSA | After Second Amendment |
|---|---|---|
| Effective Date | June 1 | June 1 |
| Due Diligence Period expires (4.1) | July 16, 5:00 pm (45th day after June 1) | August 5 |
| Closing (7.1: 30 days after expiration) | August 15 | **September 4** |

Section 7.1 does not name a date. It is a formula keyed to expiration of the Due Diligence Period, so when the amendment moved the expiration to August 5, the closing moved with it, even though the amendment says nothing about closing. August 15 is simply the original calculation (July 16 + 30) that nobody updated.

The amendment was signed July 10, before the original July 16 expiration, so it extended a live period rather than trying to revive a dead one.

## What this means for the two dates on the table

- **August 15 (title):** no longer the contract date. Nobody is obligated to close then, and the lender cannot fund by then anyway.
- **August 18 (lender):** 17 days before the contract date. It only works as the closing date if both sides agree to close early, or if the PSA gives one party a right to accelerate. As quoted, 7.1 says closing "occurs" 30 days after expiration, which is a fixed date, not an "on or before."

## Things I cannot see that could change the answer

1. **The First Amendment.** There is a Second, so there is a First. If it fixed a calendar closing date or rewrote 4.1 or 7.1, the formula may no longer control.
2. **Any hard closing date elsewhere in the PSA.** Check the definitions and basic terms page for a defined "Closing Date" stated as a calendar date, or an outside date. If one exists, there is a conflict with 7.1 that the amendment did not resolve.
3. **Time of day on August 5.** "Through August 5" has no time. The 5:00 pm cutoff in 4.1 probably carries over, but someone could argue end of day. Treat 5:00 pm as the deadline for any termination notice.
4. **Weekend and business-day rules.** You did not give the year. Check whether any of these dates falls on a weekend or holiday and whether the PSA rolls deadlines to the next business day. (If this is 2026, August 15 is a Saturday and September 4 is a Friday, the Friday before Labor Day.)
5. **How "Effective Date" and day counting are defined.** I counted June 2 as day 1, which is the normal reading of "45th day after." A different counting rule moves the original dates by a day but does not change the amended ones.
6. **Other clocks tied to the Due Diligence Period.** Deposit going hard, additional deposit, title and survey objection and cure periods, financing contingency, estoppel and SNDA delivery. All of those moved with the extension too unless they are stated as calendar dates.

## What I would do today

1. **Paper it.** A silent formula shift is exactly the kind of thing the other side disputes later, especially with earnest money at stake. Get a short Third Amendment, or at minimum a written confirmation signed by both sides, that states the Due Diligence expiration (date and time) and the Closing Date as calendar dates.
2. **Decide which date you actually want.** If August 18 suits both sides, write August 18 into that amendment. If you want the full runway, write September 4. Either way, stop relying on the formula.
3. **Check the lender's side of an August 18 versus September 4 closing:** rate lock expiration, commitment expiration, and whether third-party reports and estoppels go stale by early September.
4. **Tell title in writing** to take August 15 off the calendar and reset to the confirmed date, and to update the title commitment and proration dates accordingly.
5. **Have counsel read the full PSA and both amendments** before anyone relies on this. My read is based only on the three provisions you quoted.

Bottom line: no one is in default on August 15, and the lender's timing is not a problem. The problem is that three parties are working from three different closing dates, and the fix is one signed page that names the date.
