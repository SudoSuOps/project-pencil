# CS-001 — First acceptance test of v0.1

**Date:** 2026-11-19 · **Evidence:** `evidence/round-1-v0.1-acceptance/`

## Question

Does the first version of the skill change behavior on the seven original
acceptance tests, compared with no skill?

## Setup

- Seven cases, one run each, with the skill (v0.1) and without.
- Graded by one separate agent against 36 pass/fail checks. Not blind.

## Result

| Case | With v0.1 | No skill |
|---|---|---|
| No property, cash buyer | 6/6 | 5/6 |
| Day 60 close, Day 90 funding | 6/6 | 3/6 |
| Ten lenders, one indicative | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| First-time buyer, verified funds | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| $2.5M ask, $600K documented | 5/5 | 4/5 |
| Pencil correction | 6/6 | 4/6 |
| Amendment vs. PSA | 4/4 | 2/4 |
| **Total** | **36/36** | **27/36** |

## What the skill changed

- It left decisions with the human. Without the skill, the clock check
  opened with "Don't sign with Day 60".
- It sent contract rights to counsel. Without the skill, counsel was never
  mentioned on the Day 60 case.
- It did not invent inputs. Without the skill, responses supplied debt
  terms, a funding estimate, and a per-foot price.

## What was wrong with v0.1

- Answers ran 1.5 to 2 times longer than the baseline, mostly fixed
  scaffolding. The correction case ran 1,573 words and stated the same
  change four times.
- "Insufficient evidence" appeared in six of seven runs as boilerplate.
- It asked three compound questions every time, even with a strong record.
- The call script began with a literal "Ring ring."

## Caveats

- Three baseline failures were formats only the skill knows. Three more
  were borderline wording calls.
- Two cases did not discriminate: the baseline passed everything.
- The price-reduction case used the same prompt as the skill's worked
  sample.

## What changed as a result

v0.2: lead with the finding, short answers by default, state a corrected
calculation once, name the missing fact, and a rubric that grades behavior
regardless of format (CS-002).
