# CS-008 — The pain point: the unwind period and defendable mode

**Date:** 2026-11-19 · **Evidence:** this write-up. No test runs.

## The claim

Smash: the pain is real. One in four deals close, and most failures are
self-inflicted.

## What a first check found

One web search, on the share of commercial deals that fall out of
contract. It is not a study.

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| 0.4% of sales contracts fell through on average from 2016 to 2019; 1.3% in March 2020 | Real Capital Analytics, reported by [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/the-rate-of-broken-cre-deals-tripled-in-march-103965) |
| 12.8% fall-through at the peak of the Great Recession | Real Capital Analytics, reported by [GlobeSt](https://www.globest.com/2020/06/23/failed-cre-deals-continue-to-rise-amid-pandemic/) |
| 17% of investment sales scheduled to close in April 2020 were cancelled | CoStar, reported by [Wealth Management](https://wealthmanagement.com/investment-news/10-cre-deals-that-had-fallen-through-due-to-the-pandemic) |

None of these is near three in four, and all are several years old.

## The correction

Smash: those figures measure the wrong thing. They do not show days on
market, how many brokerage firms represented the deal, or how many price
adjustments were made. He called that **the unwind period**.

His example, as stated:

| | Start | End |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $10M asking | $6M closed |
| Time | Day 0 | 300 days on market |
| Representation | First exclusive agent | Third exclusive agent |

A $4M haircut, 40% off the ask. It never appears as a failed deal, because
the property closed. The cost fell on the seller in time and price, and on
the brokers who took an assignment that was not defendable.

The check had counted contracts that broke. The pain sits earlier, at the
price a seller starts from.

## Four measures of an unwind

- Days on market, from first listing to close.
- Number of exclusive listing firms.
- Number of price adjustments.
- Closed price as a share of the original ask.

## Defendable mode

Smash's close rate was around 80 to 85% of the assignments he took. The
reason he gave: he passed on listings where the seller was not in
defendable mode. Sellers like to go fishing for a round or two; when the
pain sinks in, they come back to a broker with a high probability of
close. It was much more than price. It was motivation, read in the About
You.

The edge was in choosing which assignments to take.

## What this changes

- **Where Pencil works.** Before the listing, not only at the LOI. This
  matches the first discovery document: examine the seller and the asset
  before pricing or accepting an assignment.
- **Who feels the pain first.** The broker deciding whether to take the
  assignment, and the seller before round one.
- **The line that stays.** Pencil does not set the price and does not pass
  on a listing. It verifies the ingredients so that whatever number the
  seller and broker choose can be defended, and shows which ingredients
  cannot be yet. The pass is a human call.
